# STORY BIBLE — PROJECT PALINODE

**Spoilers for all five chapters.** The narrative is an original fictional story written for THE MAZE: SIGNAL LOST. It is deliberately expressed through radio fragments, found recordings and environment states, leaving room for optional cutscenes and VO later.

## Logline

In 2026, Iris Vale follows a distorted call from her brother Jonah, missing since 1998, into the condemned St. Vesper Annex. The underground maze is not a physical basement. It is the architecture of a recording that has been playing backward through her life.

## Cast / voice direction

**Iris Vale** — 36, paramedic, disciplined and skeptical, struggles with an unexplainable blank in childhood. Player protagonist; rarely speaks during gameplay so players can inhabit her uncertainty. Her agency comes through the final decision, not voice-over exposition.

**Jonah Vale** — Iris's older brother, missing in 1998. The voice on Channel 07. Warm, sarcastic, exhausted. Twist: he is no longer the body that entered the building; he is a coherent recording carrying the memory Iris could not survive.

**Dr. Mara Ellis** — lead scientist of Project Palinode. Started as a clinical researcher attempting to preserve terminal patients' voices. Her mistakes created the Archive. Never flatten her into a cartoon villain: she tried to reverse the damage and eventually locked her own voice into the maintenance frequency.

**The Archivist** — the signal's defense mechanism, not a man in a costume. It adopts the form of the memories it catalogs. It wants no one to sever the carrier because interruption would end everything it holds.

**The Matron / Watcher / Siren / Husk / Crawler** — monsters manifested by fragmented patient recordings. They respond according to a single rule tied to memory: being observed, overheard, burned, retransmitted, or forgotten.

## Historical spine

- **1993–1997:** Research at St. Vesper explores recording neural echoes from dying patients. A dedicated analog archive and transmission facility is constructed underground.
- **1998:** Dr. Ellis's experiments produce an anomaly: recorded events sometimes contain future details. The archive begins to behave as a looping signal rather than a conventional recording. Eight-year-old Iris becomes a patient during an incident that the public records never acknowledge. Jonah volunteers to carry the memory imprint that is destroying Iris's mind. The facility is sealed; Jonah disappears.
- **1999–2025:** The St. Vesper site is condemned. Analog transmissions continue at 00:07. The official building inventory omits the basement. Iris grows up with a blank childhood episode and an inexplicable fear of red doorways.
- **2026:** Iris receives Channel 07. A voice calling itself Jonah describes a door below the condemned building. The player descends.

## Narrative rules (must remain consistent)

1. The building's labyrinth changes because it is a *signal structure*, not because rooms literally teleport in ordinary time.
2. Jonah is a preserved memory consciousness. The game never promises that he can regain a physical body.
3. Iris's 1998 admission explains why the archive knows her voice before her adult recordings exist.
4. Dr. Ellis's motivation was originally medical preservation, then containment; this does not excuse the sacrifices.
5. The Watcher cannot move under direct observation. Game rules must honor that mechanic without sudden arbitrary exceptions.
6. All five chapters correspond to five partitions of the same signal, arranged as intake, storage, disposal, relay, core.
7. The hidden ending uses information from *all fifteen* documents, not a random invisible percentage gate.

## Full chapter-to-chapter story map

### Act I / THE MAZE — Denial

**Question:** Who called Iris, and how did a basement appear under an empty building?

- Entry: the service lift descends after its floor indicator reaches zero, then shows a blank level.
- Echo: Jonah's voice on Channel 07 asks Iris whether she remembers the elevator.
- Core action: find three scattered memory fragments. A creature stops in the flashlight beam, but more fundamentally freezes under observation, echoing a patient study.
- Reveal: 1998 intake order, the Watcher rule, and Jonah's message referring to a time before Iris existed.
- Door scene: the first red door opens onto the impossible hospital ward. Its mechanical display says **YOU WERE HERE BEFORE**.

### Act II / THE WARD — Recognition

**Question:** Why is Iris herself listed as a patient in a hospital she has never seen?

- Entry: empty beds with warm sheets; intercom chirps, nobody answers.
- Core action: collect three patient ID bands, then start emergency power. The Matron tracks sound, turning sprint and gunfire into storytelling systems.
- Reveal: Iris's patient record lists her age as eight, her status as missing, and contradictory admission times. Jonah's letter confirms he went looking for their mother.
- Reversal: emergency power turns on devices that have apparently been waiting decades.
- Exit: a furnace gate opens below the ward.

### Act III / THE FOUNDRY — Guilt

**Question:** What did the scientists do with the memories they could no longer contain?

- Entry: incinerators without fuel, furnace ash without bodies.
- Core action: deactivate three pressure valves while armored Husks patrol.
- Reveal: Project Palinode, the false idea that memories could be incinerated, five signal partitions, Jonah's confession that the mother's recording was repeatedly altered.
- Character beat: Iris learns that what she calls “her memory” was deliberately edited for her survival.
- Exit: the furnace cools and a radio antenna can be heard rotating below the floor.

### Act IV / THE TRANSMISSION — Paradox

**Question:** Is the voice helping Iris real, or does the archive merely know how to imitate her brother?

- Entry: digital-looking interference on old analog equipment, no valid radio call signs.
- Core action: align three relays, activate a console. A Siren can disappear from view and reappear near the player's route.
- Reveal: the building is the receiver of an echo transmitted backward through time. A recording in adult Iris's voice describes the final choice. A note references a **blue monitor** inside the Heart.
- Reversal: Jonah admits that the signal may contain many voices that can imitate him, and that he himself is an imprint.
- Exit: the final connection points downward rather than toward the sky.

### Act V / THE HEART — Choice

**Question:** Should the dead be allowed a voice if it also releases everything that learned to copy them?

- Entry: the archive core is a living collage of memory segments.
- Core action: sever four memory anchors; this removes the Archivist's invulnerability. Conventional campaign: defeat it and approach transmitter. Silent Hands: completing the anchors opens a stealth bypass to the transmitter.
- Reveal: Jonah sacrificed his living future to carry Iris's memories. His message was meant to free her, not summon her back into a trap.
- Final decision: the red button offers silence or broadcast; the blue monitor offers a third way if Iris recovered all fifteen recordings.

## Endings and tonal contracts

**THE SILENCE (sever carrier):** Iris escapes. The recordings go silent. Jonah ceases to call. Quiet grief; not a jump scare.

**THE SIGNAL (broadcast):** The city hears the lost voices, including Jonah, but also every hungry copy the archive created. Wonder slides into dread; intentionally ambiguous sequel hook.

**THE REMEMBERING (collect 15 recordings):** Iris finds the blue monitor and records her own unaltered memory into the loop, so Jonah no longer has to carry it. The archive switches off. Bittersweet release; thematic closure rather than a hard sci-fi explanation.

## Radio lines / performance notes

Use sparse, close-mic tape performances, interrupted by dead air. Never over-explain while the player is being chased. Sample lines (original):

- Jonah, Chapter 1: “Iris. Three pieces. The red door only opens when you remember all of them.”
- Ellis, Chapter 2: “The ward has no discharge records. If you hear a nurse, do not answer.”
- Jonah, Chapter 3: “The fire didn't burn them. It kept them warm.”
- Iris's tape, Chapter 4: “You made this call tomorrow. Listen anyway.”
- Jonah, Chapter 5: “I am not here, Iris. I am the part of you they could not erase.”
